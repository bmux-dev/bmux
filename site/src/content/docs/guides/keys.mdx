Ctrl-a p is a last-session toggle for this client, not previous-in-list
navigation. It does nothing until you have switched from another session.
Ctrl-a ) and ( step through sessions in the sidebar's order (creation order,
with [worktree](/bmux/guides/sessions/#git-worktrees) sessions kept under their
repository) and wrap around.

Outside Vim/Neovim, bmux takes Ctrl-h/j/k/l, including in other programs such
as fzf, so by default Ctrl-l doesn't clear a shell. Inside Vim/Neovim they are
forwarded. See [editor navigation](/bmux/guides/editor-navigation/) for mappings that
cross editor edges. To forward them to other programs too, list them in
`keys.pass-through` (like vim-tmux-navigator's `is_vim` pattern); to give a
key back everywhere, [unbind it](/bmux/configuration/rebinding-keys/).

## Windows

A session holds numbered windows, like tmux's, and each window has its own
splits, focused pane and zoom. The top bar lists them after the session and
pane: the active one in the accent color, and a `!` on a background window
with a pane that needs you (a bell, a notification or a blocked agent).
A window shows its name, or until you rename it the focused pane's title, or
`shell`. Renaming to an empty name goes back to the title. Names are at most
40 bytes.

Background windows keep running at the session's size, so switching is
instant and never resizes anything. Their agents still show in the sidebar,
and opening one there switches to its window. Pane numbers are unique across
a session's windows, so `SESSION:PANE` targets and `BMUX_PANE` keep working
wherever a pane is; a bare `SESSION` target means the active window's
focused pane.

Ctrl-a & asks first when any pane in the window has a program in front of its
shell; only a lowercase y closes it. Closing a window's last pane closes the
window, and the windows after it move down a number. Leaving the active window
returns to the last window you were in. Closing the last window ends the
session. A session holds at most 32 panes across all its windows.

## Arranging panes

These follow tmux. Moving a pane never restarts it: its program, scrollback
and number go with it.

- **Ctrl-a ;** returns to the pane you were in before, in this window; press
  it again to toggle back. If that pane has closed, it does nothing.
- **Ctrl-a \{** and **\}** swap the focused pane with the previous or next pane
  (left to right, top to bottom, wrapping around), and focus stays on it.
  **Ctrl-a Ctrl-o** moves every pane one place forward. The splits stay where
  they are; only the panes trade places. Unzoom first.
- **Ctrl-a !** moves the focused pane into a new window of its own.
  **Ctrl-a @** moves it into the last window, beside that window's focused
  pane. A window left empty closes.
- **Ctrl-a Space** steps through the preset layouts: even horizontal (side by
  side), even vertical (stacked), main horizontal (the first pane across the
  top 60%, the rest below), main vertical (the first pane on the left 60%,
  the rest beside it) and tiled (a grid). **Ctrl-a Alt-1** to **Alt-5** pick
  one directly. **Ctrl-a E** keeps your splits but spreads each row or column
  evenly. Presets keep the pane order and focus, and unzoom.

## Repeat resizing

After **Ctrl-a h/j/k/l**, plain **h/j/k/l** continue resizing in any direction.
Each press renews a **one-second** window. A one-second pause, another key,
a mouse click or wheel event, paste, opening the picker, or losing terminal
focus ends repetition. The footer shows when resizing is active. Unzoom
before resizing dividers.
